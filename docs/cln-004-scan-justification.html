CLN-004 · Pre-treatment scan · Doer assistant · Checker doctor

Two people, in that order.

A radiograph may not be justified by the person who took it, and a justification written afterwards is not a justification. KuBi now refuses both.

The gap this closes

KuBi already asked for the clinical question a scan answers, and recorded who wrote it. What it never did was refuse a question written by the person who pressed the button. CLN-004 names the assistant as Doer and the doctor as Checker — two columns in the matrix, and until now one person in the software.

That is the rule sterilisation has had since it was built: an operator may not release their own batch. This is the same rule applied to a dose.

What the engine checks

Two people

Whoever justified is not whoever exposed — and the justifier is a doctor. Two assistants satisfy the letter of “two people” while answering none of the question, because deciding a dose is warranted is a clinical judgement.

In that order

The justification is timestamped before the dose. A question typed at four o’clock does not make a two o’clock exposure justified. It makes a record that says it was, which is worse than nothing, because it looks like compliance.

Both are judged strictly as of the minute of exposure, exactly as consent is. A justification that arrived afterwards is not matched to the exposure at all — matching it would be the engine doing the retro-fitting it exists to prevent. And a failure is permanent: nothing written later clears it, because the patient has already had the dose.

Every verdict the engine can reach

VerdictWhenWhy it is its own verdict
JUSTIFIED A doctor wrote a real question before the dose, and did not take it. The only one that is not a finding.
UNJUSTIFIED Nothing was written at all. A gap in the record.
NO_QUESTION Something was written and it says nothing. “Routine” is not a question. A record that is wrong, not missing. Different fix, different person.
SELF_JUSTIFIED The person who wrote it took it. Doer and Checker collapsed into one.
NOT_A_DOCTOR Two people, neither of them clinically able to make the call. Two people is not the whole rule.
JUSTIFIED_AFTER Written after the dose. To a regulator this is the same as nothing. Inside a clinic it is a completely different conversation.

Five of the six are permanent. A dose was given; the record does not stand up; and the only honest thing the software can do is keep saying so.

The half that prevents rather than records

A register that reports a self-justified scan a week later has helped nobody. So the same rules run before the button is offered — at the moment somebody could still walk down the corridor and fetch the doctor. If Priya asks to take Farah Qureshi’s periapical on her own justification, the screen does not show her a button:

Refused Owner · treating doctor

You wrote the justification for this exposure, so you may not also take it. CLN-004 names the assistant as Doer and the doctor as Checker, and one person cannot be both.

The refusal names who can clear it, so the sentence ends somewhere useful.

What this still does not settle

Three questions the matrix does not answer. KuBi has behaviour for each of them and nobody has ratified it, so each is recorded as an open owner decision rather than quietly resolved.

  1. How long may a justification stand before the exposure? A question written at Monday’s consultation is a reasonable basis for Tuesday’s radiograph and probably not for one in six weeks. KuBi does not expire them.

  2. May one justification cover a series — four bitewings, or a trauma follow-up sequence? KuBi matches each exposure to the most recent justification before it, so one covers many, and nobody has said whether that is right.

  3. Who justifies when the doctor taking the radiograph is the only clinician in the building? CLN-004 assumes two people are available, and a single-handed session is the case where the rule bites hardest.

Where it lives

  • packages/contracts/src/radiography.ts The engine. Pure: exposures and justifications in, verdicts out. No clock, no database.
  • packages/contracts/src/clinical.ts CLN-004 reads its verdict from the register rather than from a tick.
  • apps/web/src/screens/clinical.tsx The Scans tab, drawing the same sequence this page draws.
  • tests/unit/radiography.test.ts Twenty-seven tests: two people, that order, refusing before the button, the register across a day, reading the log.

Two events carry it — a justification and an exposure — each holding booking, employee and role in its subject, because a readiness event carries no payload. The clinical question is prose and travels beside them, keyed by the same minute.

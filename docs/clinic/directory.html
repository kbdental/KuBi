Section 1

Stakeholders

Seven roles touch the day. Roles, never names — names can change but roles do not change. The log still records which employee did what; that is provenance, not the interface.

Enforced, not advisory

A role that does not own a block cannot report it. The server refuses, in the person's own words: "Cleaning the floors and shared areas is not your part of the morning." This was found by driving a real morning — an assistant recorded housekeeping's floors and the clinic accepted it, which turned the whole readiness calculation into a formality.

Senior Assistant counts as an assistant for rooms and stock and nowhere else. The equipment round is theirs alone, and releasing a sterilisation batch stays a separation-of-duties gate so that nobody releases their own work.

Four rooms come from the master, not from the code

A fifth operatory is a row in a table, not a release. And what is checked in a room is fixed at four things — dental chair, x-ray, hand instruments, suction — because the special kit travels on a trolley and is the equipment round instead. Buying a second scanner does not change the shape of anybody's morning.

Ready is calculated, never ticked

ROOMS_READY is refused until every block underneath it has actually been reported. Nobody can assert a ready clinic; they can only do the work that makes it one.

Staff entry is counted, not gated — and that is deliberate

The hygiene protocol gates each person, and an event here carries the role that recorded it rather than the individual. Counting is honest; refusing would not be, because the model cannot yet tell two assistants apart. Recorded as a known limit rather than quietly enforced on a role.

Section 3 · Clinic closing

The evening

30 minutes from the moment the clinic shuts. Shut time is 18:30, with exception days at 19:00 — both are data, because a clinic that only knew 18:30 would call every one of those evenings an overrun by the team that stayed late.

Ordered by owner rather than interleaved: two people working at once is what makes thirty minutes possible, and a list that alternates between them reads like a queue when it is not one.

All eleven hold the lockup

Patient safety decides which one the manager is told about first — not whether it can be skipped. There is no override path and no acknowledgement, on your ruling.

The refusal is ordered by what matters

Locking the day is refused on four things in sequence: an unfinished visit → an unwritten clinical note → an autoclave cycle that ran and was never released → the closing drill. A manager who left a cycle unreleased is told about the cycle, not about the bins.

Same-day sterilisation is not reachable, and the arithmetic says so

Cycle 75 minutes; the team leaves 30 minutes after the door shuts. The last instrument that could be stored today would have to be collected at 17:45 — forty-five minutes before the clinic shuts. Your ruling: the morning run catches yesterday's instruments, and the safety net sits at opening, where the clinic cannot be called ready until a run is released. A batch waiting overnight is the normal end of a day.

The log cannot be edited, by grant rather than by convention

Section 5 · What is missing

The gaps

5a · Opening — six controls, one missing block

Six of the seven uncovered controls are one thing wearing six matrix rows: opening the building. Neither document has that section. Your closing drill has its exact mirror image — Clinic Environment Closing switches all of it off in the evening — and the opening procedure begins with people already inside a working building. The clinic plainly does this every morning. KuBi has no block for it, and nobody has said whose job it is.

  • OPEN-001Open the second-floor clinic
  • OPEN-007ACs at 24 °C
  • OPEN-008Air diffuser
  • OPEN-009Water availability
  • OPEN-010Water pump — "starting the water pump is a task of opening"
  • OPEN-011Lights and fans
The one answer that closes all six: who opens the building? Housekeeping arrive first for the forty-five minutes of mopping, which makes them the obvious candidate — but that is a guess, and a guessed owner is a block that gets reported by whoever happens to be standing there. Name the role and this becomes a single PREMISES_OPENED block at the head of the morning.
5b · The most serious gap on this page

OPEN-012 — morning emergency readiness

The emergency kit, the drugs and their expiry dates, the oxygen. This is patient safety, and it is absent from the activity matrix's detail, from the opening procedure, and from every block KuBi holds. It sits in the same uncovered list as the fans and the ACs and it is not the same kind of thing — a gap that reads like a light switch will be filled like one, which is why it is on its own here.

Two answers needed: who checks it, and what does the check consist of — is it a daily look at the kit, or a dated expiry register? The second answer decides whether this is a block or a register with its own review cycle.
5c · Documents still owed

The K. B. Dental Hand Hygiene Protocol — "will provide later". The staff-entry gate has no standard behind it: the twenty-second handwash is currently a step with nothing to check it against. The Five Step Sterilization Protocol is closed; the sixteen steps stand as written.

5d · Two statements that disagree

Neither can be resolved by reading harder — both statements are in the opening document.

  • 1Reception floor agent. The 3-bucket technique says Virulex; the reception paragraph says 1 % sodium hypochlorite.
  • 2Eye shield. PPE says disinfect and reuse; BMW says red bin as contaminated plastic. Both can be true only if these are two different items.
  • 3Needles. The formalin chamber lists needles; BMW puts them in the white sharps box, never recapped. Sterilising and discarding the same item cannot both be the rule.
  • 4Fumigation cadence. "Once every day" and "on a holiday or end of the day" are not the same schedule.
5e · Ordering the documents do not state
  • 5Floors before surfaces, or after?
  • 6Equipment check before the sterilisation run, or after? The autoclave is checked in §3 and used in §7.
  • 7Where does the chemical route run? The sixteen steps end in an autoclave; the chemical-route instruments presumably go to the formalin chamber, but no sequence is given.
5f · One model limit, raised rather than worked around

Per-patient sterilisation has one owner and you have named two. The flow gives each step a single owning role, so the turnover run displays as the technician's even when a dental assistant does it. Nothing is refused — role ownership is enforced only on the readiness and closing blocks — but the displayed owner is narrower than the clinic. Widening it means letting a node have two owners, which changes the frozen model, so it is raised rather than done.

Section 7

Settled — so it is not reopened by accident

How many operatories?
Four, from a master. A fifth is a row, not a release.
Technician or assistant on sterilisation?
The technician owns the morning run; either does the per-patient runs.
Who owns each block?
Rooms and stock → Assistant or Senior · equipment round → Senior only · waiting and billing → Reception · floors → Housekeeping · morning run → Technician.
The two unknown durations
The equipment round and the waiting area happen inside the fifty minutes. They carry no clock of their own, which is different from unknown.
Is the first appointment the target?
Yes. A day with nothing booked reports no target rather than an invented one.
Total readiness minutes
50, excluding the sterilisation cycle.
Total closing minutes
30, from the moment the clinic shuts.
Clinic shut time
18:30, exception days 19:00 — both held as data.
Refuse, or acknowledge, at lockup?
Keep refusing. No override path.
Same-day sterilisation?
No. The morning run catches yesterday's instruments.
Fumigation
Daily at end of day. Not the long pole — the hour the rooms stay shut is spent with nobody in the building.
Names in the interface?
Roles only.
